# **I. CHARACTER’S CORPORATE STRUCTURE AND HISTORY**

# **II. CIRCUMSTANCES GIVING RISE TO THIS CHAPTER 11 CASE** 

25. As previously discussed, Character has faced declining liquidity and high costs from operating its business and defending various lawsuits and regulatory investigations relating largely to safety concerns surrounding Character’s products. 

# **A. Declining Liquidity** 

26. At all times, Character has struggled with profitability and its operational expenditure has been high. For example, in the second half of 2024, following the 2024 Transaction, Character’s operating expenses (excluding legal fees) averaged approximately $21.9 million _per month_ , including monthly compute costs of approximately $13.8 million. In fiscal year 2025, operating expenses (excluding legal fees) averaged approximately $18.5 million _per month_ , including monthly compute costs of approximately $10.0 million. 

27. Character has, however, taken significant steps to reduce its operating burn, including transitioning inference workloads to lower-cost GPU capacity and post-training openweight models rather than developing proprietary foundation models. At the same time, Character reduced its employee headcount and consolidated its facilities. As a result, operating expenses (excluding legal fees) had declined to approximately $13.3 million by August 2026, a reduction of approximately 39% from the second half of 2024 monthly average and approximately 28% from the 2025 monthly average. Finally, net cash burn per month had declined to approximately $8.2 million by August 2026, compared with monthly averages of approximately $21.5 million in the second half of 2024 and approximately $17.6 million in 2025. 

28. Character has also worked to build and diversify revenue, which it primarily derives from subscriptions and in-app advertising. Monthly subscription revenue was approximately $0.6

million in January 2024, but due to increased marketing and other efforts, by August of 2026, this revenue stream was $2.8 million. In mid-2025, Character launched in-app advertising and partnered with multiple advertising networks to generate additional revenue sources. In March 2026, Character entered into an amended programmatic revenue-share and incentive arrangement with a leading online advertisement provider. Monthly advertising revenue grew from approximately $0.5 million in July 2025, its first full month, to approximately $2.5 million in August 2026. In early 2026, Character also introduced in-app purchases. As a result of all these efforts, total revenue grew from approximately $7.4 million in fiscal year 2024 to approximately $23.6 million in fiscal year 2025.

# **C. Goal of the Case** 

48. Character intends to use the Chapter 11 Case as a forum to negotiate a consensual resolution with the Multistate Group, personal injury claimants, and other creditor groups, while maximizing the value of Character’s assets for the benefit of all of the Debtor’s stakeholders. 

49. The first step to achieving this goal is the sale process that will be led by Houlihan. The sale process builds on the past marketing efforts of the Prior Banker and Character, while

casting a wide net for potential purchasers of Character’s assets. The first step is already on solid footing in light of the proposed transaction with Disney and the associated capital infusion and workforce benefits that have already been provided to the Debtor by Disney’s accommodations. Character hopes that the sale process will yield a higher or better or additional proposal that will facilitate continued operations of the Character.AI platform. If such a bid is unavailable, Character’s platform will be wound down. 

50. The second step in Character’s efforts to maximize value is to implement a mechanism that will allow the Debtor to propose a chapter 11 plan of liquidation expeditiously, enabling the Character estate to make distributions to creditors in the near term.  While the Character estate represents a classic “melting ice cube” scenario under which a mostly static pool of cash liquidity is reduced by the costs to operate as a chapter 11 debtor and prosecute these cases, reasonable scenario modeling and projections assuming a sales process consistent with the timing proposed by the Debtor and a case unburdened by contentious litigation, show that the Debtor will be able to pay administrative claims as they come due and that the Debtor can reasonably expect to end the first quarter of 2027 with a positive cash balance.  In this regard, Character, while taking advantage of the “breathing room” afforded by the automatic stay under section 362 of the Bankruptcy Code, is hopeful to bring all creditors to a single forum for global negotiations.
